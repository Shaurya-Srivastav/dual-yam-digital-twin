Two arms. One dispenser. No cameras.

A MuJoCo digital twin of a two-arm I2RT YAM cup station. One arm brings a paper or plastic cup under the spout, the other lifts a 360° lever for a timed 7-second pour, and the cup is served into a coaster. Every skill works from joint position, velocity, torque and gripper width alone.

    How it works

    From joint torque to a served cup

    Six layers, and only the last one may look at where things really are. Click a layer to see what it does.

    The one-arm flip

    Grasp upside down, roll the wrist back, pour.
    • The cup hand arrives with its wrist rolled half a turn the other way and closes on the middle of the inverted top cup, the same band an upright cup is carried by.
    • The other arm pins the stack while the top cup is wiggled and pulled free. The pull force collapsing is the signal that the nested cups let go.
    • The hand rises clear and rolls the wrist back to its normal position. The cup comes up upright and goes straight to the spout. It is never handed over or set down.
    • A level hand-up grasp at the stack would put wrist joint 5 on its stop, so the grasp dips 8°. That leaves a 16° lean after the roll, which the carry to the spout takes out.

    Finding the lever without moving it

    Close on the knob, then close around the circle.
    • The tap arm closes on the handle with the jaws across it. A stall at 15 mm, the width of the paddle, means that radius is square. A wider stall is only a graze and is not trusted.
    • The fingers open to 32 mm beside the handle, so the pads stay off the tank. Handles the wrist can square are turned to the pour angle and lifted.
    • A handle the forward pinch cannot reach is felt as a narrow stall on the reverse side. A short push is supposed to walk it into the pour sector. That push is in this build and has not been scored on 24 trials. A long fingertip sweep is not used.

    Finding the cup without cameras

    Stomp each half of the mat with the pads flat.
    • Both grippers open and lay the black pads flat, parallel to the mat. The contact is the whole pad, not a fingertip.
    • The cup arm stomps the +y half. The tap arm stomps the −y half. A stomp that stops high has a cup under the pads. One that reaches the mat rules those centres out.
    • The map is the cup centres still consistent with the pad footprints. The next stomp splits what is left.

    A side grasp that does not push the cup

    The stall width says how far off-centre the hand is.
    • The open jaws slide in and comply sideways to contact force, so the funnel pads centre the hand on the cup.
    • When the jaws stall, the width is the chord 2√(r²−s²), which gives the offset s from the cup's axis. Depth is corrected from that alone.
    • Each grip has a force cap. Nothing is welded or snapped into place.

    Research

    The papers behind the blind skills

    What the twin took from each paper, and where it does something simpler. Full notes are in docs/RESEARCH.md.

    Finding a cup by touch

    1. Memory Unscented Particle Filter for 6-DOF Tactile Localization
      Vezzani, Pattacini, Battistelli, Chisci, Natale · IEEE T-RO 2017 · arXiv. A belief over object pose, updated from contact.
    2. Information Gain Criteria for Active Tactile Action Selection
      Murali, Dahiya, Kaboli · 2022. Choose the next touch by how much it should shrink the belief.
    3. Touch-Based Object Localisation with Spatially-Aware Belief Entropy
      Brudermüller, Jankowski, Toussaint, Hawes · ICRA 2026. Touch-only global localisation, then a grasp.
    4. Tac2Pose: Tactile Object Pose Estimation from the First Touch
      Bauza, Bronars, Rodriguez · IJRR 2023 · arXiv. A grid of poses, each paired with the contact it would make.
    What runs here: both grippers stomp with the black pads flat, one half of the mat each. A grid of cup centres is kept or ruled out from the pad footprint. It is not a particle filter, it does not compute expected entropy, and it does not use the Tac2Pose embedding.

    Finding a handle without moving it

    1. Grasp State Estimation Using Tendon-Based Proprioception
      Lee, Park, Cho · 2025. Pause the first finger that touches, so the object is not pushed before the grasp is set.
    2. Revisiting Proprioceptive Sensing for Articulated Object Manipulation
      Lips, wyffels · ICRA 2023 workshop. Estimate a joint from the gripper pose while in contact.
    3. Contact State Estimation using Machine Learning
      Jamali, Kormushev, Caldwell · OCEANS 2013. Classify contact on a T-bar valve from force and torque.
    What runs here: the jaw width. A 15 mm stall is a square pinch and is turned to the pour angle. A narrower stall only says the paddle was touched. A short push is meant to walk that handle into reach. No learned classifier, and no fingertip sweep.

    The live dashboard
